📝 WEEK 3 — Important Questions & Answers:-

🔹 Short Questions:-

1. What is a habit?

Answer:-

A habit is a regular behaviour or activity that a person performs repeatedly, often without giving it much conscious thought. Habits develop through repeated actions over time.

For example, brushing your teeth every morning, studying at a fixed time, exercising regularly, or checking your phone after waking up can become habits.

In simple words, a habit is something we repeatedly do until it becomes a natural part of our daily life.

2. How are habits formed?

Answer:-

Habits are formed through repeated behaviour. When a person performs the same action again and again in a particular situation, the brain gradually begins to associate that situation with the action.

The basic process of habit formation is:

Cue → Routine → Reward.

For example, when a student feels tired while studying (cue), they may check social media (routine) and feel entertained or relaxed (reward). Repeating this process can turn it into a habit.

Therefore, regular repetition, a suitable environment and a rewarding experience play important roles in habit formation.

3. What are good habits?

Answer:-

Good habits are positive behaviours that improve a person's health, knowledge, character, productivity and overall development.

Examples of good habits include:-

  • Studying regularly.
  • Waking up on time.
  • Maintaining personal hygiene.
  • Exercising regularly.
  • Reading books.
  • Eating healthy food.
  • Managing time properly.
  • Being honest.
  • Respecting others.
  • Completing work on time.

Good habits help a person become disciplined, responsible and productive.

4. What are bad habits?

Answer:-

Bad habits are repeated behaviours that can negatively affect a person's health, studies, work, relationships or personal development.

Examples include:-

  • Procrastination.
  • Excessive use of mobile phones.
  • Sleeping very late.
  • Skipping important work.
  • Smoking or other harmful behaviours.
  • Wasting time.
  • Being dishonest.
  • Poor time management.
  • Avoiding responsibilities.

Bad habits may provide temporary comfort or pleasure but can create problems when they become regular and difficult to control.

5. What is a habit audit?

Answer:-

A habit audit is the process of examining and reviewing one's daily habits to identify which habits are helpful, harmful or unnecessary.

A person can make a list of their daily activities and classify them into:

  • Good habits.
  • Bad habits.
  • Neutral or unnecessary habits.

For example, a student may discover that studying for one hour daily is a good habit, while spending several hours unnecessarily scrolling through social media is an unhelpful habit.

A habit audit helps a person understand their behaviour and decide which habits should be developed, changed or removed.

6. What is the habit cycle?

Answer:-

The habit cycle is a pattern through which habits are created and repeated. It generally consists of three main elements:

Cue → Routine → Reward.

Cue:- The trigger that starts the behaviour.

Routine:- The behaviour or action performed.

Reward:- The benefit or feeling received after performing the action.

For example:-

Cue:- Feeling bored
↓
Routine:- Watching short videos
↓
Reward:- Entertainment

When this cycle is repeated regularly, the behaviour may become a habit.

7. What is a cue?

Answer:-

A cue is a trigger or signal that reminds or encourages a person to perform a particular behaviour.

A cue can be:-

  • A particular time.
  • A place.
  • An emotion.
  • Another person.
  • A previous activity.

For example, seeing your study table may remind you to study. In this case, the study table acts as a cue.

8. What is a routine?

Answer:-

A routine is the actual behaviour or action that a person performs after a cue.

For example, if a student sees their study timetable and starts studying, studying is the routine.

In the habit cycle:-

Cue → Routine → Reward.

The routine is therefore the central action that is repeated and can eventually become a habit.

9. What is a reward?

Answer:-

A reward is the positive result, benefit or satisfying feeling that a person receives after performing a behaviour.

For example, a student studies for one hour and then takes a short break. The feeling of relaxation after completing the study session can act as a reward.

Rewards can encourage a person to repeat a behaviour and can therefore contribute to habit formation.

10. How can bad habits be broken?

Answer:-

Bad habits can be broken by identifying the habit, understanding its trigger and gradually replacing the unwanted behaviour with a healthier alternative.

Some useful steps are:-

  1. Identify the bad habit.
  2. Find the cue or trigger.
  3. Understand the reward associated with the habit.
  4. Replace the routine with a healthier behaviour.
  5. Avoid situations that strongly trigger the habit when possible.
  6. Practise the new behaviour repeatedly.
  7. Track progress.
  8. Learn from mistakes instead of giving up.
  9. Stay consistent and patient.

For example, instead of checking social media whenever feeling bored, a person can replace that routine with reading a few pages of a book or taking a short walk.

11. What is the Zeigarnik Effect?

Answer:-

The Zeigarnik Effect refers to the tendency to remember unfinished or interrupted tasks more strongly than completed tasks.

For example, if a student starts an assignment but leaves it incomplete, the unfinished task may continue to occupy the student's thoughts.

This idea can be useful for productivity because starting a task can create a mental reminder to return to it and complete it.

12. How can the Zeigarnik Effect improve productivity?

Answer:-

The Zeigarnik Effect can be used as a productivity technique by starting important tasks and clearly identifying what remains unfinished.

For example, instead of simply thinking, "I have to study," a student can start a chapter and stop at a clearly defined point, such as after completing two sections. The unfinished task may remain mentally noticeable, making it easier to return to it later.

It can help a person:-

  • Remember unfinished tasks.
  • Reduce the chance of forgetting important work.
  • Maintain focus on pending activities.
  • Break large tasks into smaller parts.
  • Encourage task completion.

However, having too many unfinished tasks can also become stressful, so tasks should be organised and completed systematically.

13. What are success habits?

Answer:-

Success habits are positive and repeated behaviours that help a person achieve personal, academic or professional goals.

Examples include:-

  • Setting clear goals.
  • Managing time effectively.
  • Studying regularly.
  • Reading and learning continuously.
  • Exercising.
  • Maintaining discipline.
  • Planning daily activities.
  • Completing tasks on time.
  • Reviewing progress.
  • Learning from mistakes.

Success habits do not guarantee success by themselves, but they can support consistent effort and personal development.

14. Why is consistency important in habit formation?

Answer:-

Consistency is important because habits become stronger through repeated practice.

If a person performs a desired behaviour regularly, it gradually becomes more familiar and easier to perform.

For example, studying for 30 minutes every day can help create a regular study habit. Studying for five hours one day and then doing nothing for several days is generally less consistent.

Consistency helps to:-

  • Strengthen routines.
  • Build discipline.
  • Reduce dependence on motivation.
  • Make behaviours more automatic.
  • Support long-term goals.

15. What is self-discipline?

Answer:-

Self-discipline is the ability to control one's behaviour, emotions and actions so that one can continue working toward a goal even when one does not feel motivated.

For example, a student may prefer to use their phone, but they put the phone aside and study because they have decided to complete their work.

Self-discipline helps a person:-

  • Control distractions.
  • Follow routines.
  • Manage time.
  • Complete responsibilities.
  • Maintain consistency.
  • Work toward long-term goals.

1. Define habit and explain the principles of developing good habits.

Answer:-

A habit is a regular behaviour or activity that a person performs repeatedly until it becomes a natural or automatic part of daily life.

Good habits are developed gradually through repeated positive actions. The following principles are important for developing good habits:

  1. Start Small:-

    A new habit should begin with a simple and manageable action. For example, instead of deciding to study for five hours every day, a student can begin with 30 minutes.

  2. Be Consistent:-

    The behaviour should be repeated regularly. Consistency helps make the behaviour more familiar.

  3. Set Clear Goals:-

    A person should know exactly what they want to achieve. A clear goal provides direction.

  4. Use a Cue:-

    A specific time, place or activity can be used as a reminder for the new habit. For example, studying immediately after dinner can create a regular pattern.

  5. Create a Suitable Environment:-

    The environment should support the desired habit. For example, keeping books ready on the study table can make studying easier.

  6. Reward Progress:-

    Small rewards can provide positive reinforcement and encourage continuation.

  7. Track Progress:-

    Recording daily progress can help a person see improvement and identify problems.

  8. Be Patient:-

    Habits do not usually develop instantly. Regular practice over time is important.

Conclusion:-

Good habits are developed through small actions, clear goals, repetition, consistency, self-discipline and patience.

4. Discuss the steps involved in breaking bad habits.

Answer:-

Breaking a bad habit requires awareness, planning, replacement and consistent practice. The following steps can help:

Step 1:- Identify the Habit:-

First, clearly identify the behaviour that needs to be changed.

Step 2:- Identify the Cue:-

Find out what triggers the behaviour. For example, boredom, stress, a particular place or a particular time may trigger a habit.

Step 3:- Understand the Reward:-

Identify what benefit the person receives from the behaviour, such as entertainment, relaxation or temporary relief.

Step 4:- Replace the Routine:-

Instead of simply trying to stop the behaviour, replace it with a healthier alternative.

For example:-

Boredom → Social media → Entertainment

can be changed to:

Boredom → Reading → Learning/Entertainment

Step 5:- Remove Triggers Where Possible:-

A person can reduce exposure to situations or objects that strongly encourage the unwanted behaviour.

Step 6:- Track Progress:-

Keeping a record can help identify improvement and setbacks.

Step 7:- Practise Self-Discipline:-

The person needs to make conscious decisions and continue following the new behaviour.

Step 8:- Learn From Relapses:-

Making a mistake does not mean that all progress is lost. The person should identify what caused the setback and continue.

Conclusion:-

Breaking bad habits is a gradual process. Awareness, replacement, consistency, self-discipline and patience are important.

5. What is the Zeigarnik Effect? Explain how it can be used for productivity and personal growth.

Answer:-

The Zeigarnik Effect is the idea that people may remember unfinished or interrupted tasks more strongly than completed tasks.

For example, when a student starts reading a chapter but leaves it unfinished, the incomplete chapter may remain in the student's thoughts.

Using the Zeigarnik Effect for Productivity

Start Important Tasks:-

Starting a task can create a mental reminder to return to it.

Break Large Tasks into Smaller Tasks:-

A large project can be divided into smaller unfinished steps.

Write Down Pending Tasks:-

A task list can make unfinished work clear and organised.

Set Clear Next Steps:-

Before stopping work, decide exactly what you will do next.

Use It to Maintain Momentum:-

Leaving a task at a clear stopping point can make it easier to resume later.

Personal Growth:-

The effect can encourage people to continue working toward unfinished goals, learning activities and personal projects.

However, having too many unfinished tasks may create mental pressure. Therefore, tasks should be prioritised and completed systematically.

Conclusion:-

The Zeigarnik Effect can be used as a productivity aid by making unfinished work visible and creating clear next steps.

8. Explain how consistency, self-discipline and goal-setting help in habit formation.

Answer:-

Consistency, self-discipline and goal-setting are closely connected with successful habit formation.

1. Consistency:-

Consistency means performing a behaviour regularly over time.

For example, studying for 30 minutes every evening creates a regular study pattern. Repeated practice makes the behaviour more familiar.

2. Self-Discipline:-

Self-discipline is the ability to continue following a planned behaviour even when a person does not feel motivated.

For example, a student may feel like using their phone instead of studying, but self-discipline helps them follow their study schedule.

3. Goal-Setting:-

Goal-setting gives a person a clear purpose.

For example:-

Goal: Improve English vocabulary.
Action: Learn 10 new words every day.

The goal explains what the person wants to achieve, while the daily action helps create the habit.

How They Work Together

Clear Goal → Regular Action → Consistency → Self-Discipline → Stronger Habit

For example, a student wants to improve mathematics.

Goal:- Improve mathematics.
Plan:- Practise mathematics for 30 minutes daily.
Consistency:- Practise every day.
Self-discipline:- Continue even when motivation is low.
Result:- Regular practice gradually becomes a study habit.

Conclusion:-

Goal-setting provides direction, consistency provides repetition, and self-discipline helps maintain the behaviour. Together, they support the development of strong and useful habits.
