📝 WEEK 2 — Important Questions & Answers:-

🔹 Short Questions:-

1. What is conflict?

Answer:-
Conflict is a disagreement or difference of opinions, interests, needs, or ideas between two or more people or groups. In simple words, conflict happens when people do not agree with each other.

2. What is conflict resolution?

Answer:-
Conflict resolution is the process of finding a peaceful and effective solution to a disagreement or conflict. Its aim is to solve the problem and maintain a healthy relationship between the people involved.

3. What is a win-win solution?

Answer:-
A win-win solution is a solution in which both sides have their important needs and interests considered and both sides gain something. It aims to benefit both parties rather than allowing only one side to win.

4. Mention four causes of conflict.

Answer:-
Four common causes of conflict are:

  • Difference in opinions.
  • Miscommunication.
  • Different interests.
  • Lack of understanding.

5. What is interpersonal conflict?

Answer:-
Interpersonal conflict is a disagreement or conflict between two or more individuals. It may occur between friends, family members, students, teachers and students, or colleagues.

6. What is negotiation?

Answer:-
Negotiation is the process of discussing a problem or disagreement with another person or group in order to reach a mutually acceptable agreement.

7. What is collaboration?

Answer:-
Collaboration is the process of working together with others to solve a problem or achieve a common goal. It involves cooperation, communication, mutual respect, and shared responsibility.

8. What is intrapersonal conflict?

Answer:-
Intrapersonal conflict is a conflict that occurs within a person's own mind. It happens when a person has two or more opposing thoughts, desires, feelings, or choices.

Example:- A student wants to study for an examination but also wants to play games.

9. What is intragroup conflict?

Answer:-
Intragroup conflict is a conflict that occurs between members of the same group.

Example:- Members of a project team disagree about how to complete their project.

10. What is intergroup conflict?

Answer:-
Intergroup conflict is a conflict that occurs between two or more groups.

Example:- Two departments of an organisation disagree about the allocation of resources.

11. Define stress.

Answer:-
Stress is a physical, mental, or emotional response to demanding or challenging situations. It occurs when a person feels that the demands of a situation are difficult to handle.

12. What is eustress?

Answer:-
Eustress is a form of positive or useful stress that motivates a person to perform better, take action, and achieve goals.

Example:- A little nervousness before an examination can motivate a student to prepare well.

13. What is distress?

Answer:-
Distress is harmful or excessive stress that negatively affects a person's physical, mental, or emotional well-being and may reduce performance.

Example:- Constant worry about examinations may cause lack of sleep and poor concentration.

14. What is acute stress?

Answer:-
Acute stress is short-term stress that occurs because of a specific event or situation.

Example:- Feeling nervous before giving a presentation.

15. What is chronic stress?

Answer:-
Chronic stress is long-term stress that continues for a prolonged period, such as weeks, months, or longer.

Example:- Continuous pressure at work for several months.

16. What is stress regulation?

Answer:-
Stress regulation means managing and controlling one's response to stressful situations so that stress does not negatively affect physical, mental, and emotional well-being.

17. Mention four signs of stress.

Answer:-
Four common signs of stress are:

  • Headache.
  • Tiredness.
  • Irritability.
  • Poor concentration.

18. How can stress be managed?

Answer:-
Stress can be managed through:-

  • Proper time management.
  • Regular exercise.
  • Adequate sleep.
  • Relaxation and deep breathing.
  • Positive thinking.
  • Social support.
  • Breaking large tasks into smaller parts.
  • Maintaining a healthy lifestyle.

⭐ Long Questions:-

1. Explain the concept of conflict resolution and discuss the importance of a win-win solution.

Answer:-
Conflict resolution is the process of finding a peaceful and effective solution to a disagreement or conflict. Conflicts may occur because of differences in opinions, interests, needs, expectations, communication, or values.

The purpose of conflict resolution is not simply to prove who is right or wrong. Its main purpose is to understand the problem, respect the people involved, and find a solution that can improve the situation and maintain a healthy relationship.

Steps in conflict resolution:-

  1. Identify the problem:— Understand what the actual conflict is.
  2. Listen to both sides:- — Give both parties an opportunity to explain their views.
  3. Identify needs and interests:- — Understand what each person actually needs.
  4. Avoid blaming:- — Focus on solving the problem rather than blaming someone.
  5. Generate possible solutions:- — Think of different ways to solve the conflict.
  6. Evaluate the solutions:- — Consider which solution is acceptable to both sides.
  7. Agree on a solution:- — Select a mutually acceptable solution.
  8. Implement and review:- — Put the solution into practice and check whether it works.

Importance of a win-win solution:-

A win-win solution is one in which both parties benefit from the outcome. It is important because it:-

  • Satisfies the important needs of both sides.
  • Reduces hostility and anger.
  • Builds trust and mutual respect.
  • Maintains healthy relationships.
  • Encourages cooperation.
  • Provides a long-term solution to conflicts.

Therefore, a win-win approach is useful because it tries to solve the problem without creating a new problem between the people involved.

2. What is interpersonal conflict? Explain its causes with suitable examples.

Answer:-
Interpersonal conflict is a disagreement or conflict between two or more individuals. It can occur between friends, family members, students, teachers and students, colleagues, managers and employees, etc.

Causes of interpersonal conflict:-

1. Poor communication:-
When people do not communicate clearly, misunderstandings can develop.

Example:- Two students misunderstand each other's instructions while working on a project.

2. Difference in opinions:-
People may have different ideas about how something should be done.

Example:- Two friends disagree about which plan they should follow.

3. Different expectations:-
Conflict may occur when people expect different things from each other.

Example:- One student expects both members to contribute equally to a project, while the other does not.

4. Competition:-
Competition for the same opportunity or resource can create conflict.

Example:- Two employees want the same position or workplace resource.

5. Lack of trust:-
When people do not trust each other, misunderstandings and disagreements may increase.

6. Personality differences:-
People have different personalities, habits, and ways of thinking, which can sometimes cause conflict.

Thus, effective communication, understanding, cooperation, and mutual respect are important for reducing interpersonal conflicts.

3. Explain different ways of resolving interpersonal conflicts.

Answer:- Interpersonal conflicts can be resolved through several effective approaches.

1. Communication:-

People should communicate openly and clearly about the problem. They should explain their views respectfully instead of reacting with anger.

2. Active listening:-

Each person should listen carefully to the other person's point of view without interrupting. This helps reduce misunderstandings.

3. Negotiation:-
Negotiation involves discussing the problem and trying to reach an agreement that is acceptable to both sides.

4. Collaboration:-
Collaboration means working together to find a solution. Instead of competing against each other, both sides cooperate to achieve a common goal.

5. Empathy:-
Empathy means trying to understand another person's feelings and point of view. It helps people understand why the other person is behaving in a particular way.

6. Mutual respect:-
Even during disagreement, people should respect each other's opinions, feelings, and rights.

7. Focus on the problem, not the person:-
People should try to solve the actual problem instead of blaming or attacking each other.

Therefore, communication, active listening, negotiation, collaboration, empathy, and mutual respect can help resolve interpersonal conflicts effectively.

4. Discuss the different types of conflicts with examples.

Answer:-
There are several major types of conflicts:

1. Intrapersonal Conflict:-
It occurs within an individual. A person may have opposing thoughts, desires, or decisions.

Example:- A student wants to study but also wants to spend time playing games.

2. Interpersonal Conflict:-
It occurs between two or more individuals.

Example:- Two friends disagree about an important decision.

3. Intragroup Conflict:-
It occurs between members of the same group.

Example:- Members of a project team disagree about how to complete their project.

4. Intergroup Conflict:-
It occurs between two or more groups.

Example:- Two departments disagree about the distribution of resources.

5. Organisational Conflict:-
It occurs within an organisation because of differences in goals, responsibilities, policies, interests, or resources.

Example:- Employees and management disagree about work responsibilities or organisational policies.

Understanding the type of conflict helps people choose an appropriate method to resolve it.

5. What skills are required to become an effective conflict-resolution expert?

Answer:-
A person needs several important skills to become effective at conflict resolution.

  1. Active Listening:- A person should listen carefully without interrupting and understand what others are saying.
  2. Empathy:- Empathy helps a person understand the feelings and perspectives of others.
  3. Patience:- A person should remain calm and avoid reacting immediately in anger.
  4. Effective Communication:- Ideas, feelings, and concerns should be expressed clearly and respectfully.
  5. Problem-Solving:- The focus should be on finding practical solutions rather than blaming people.
  6. Emotional Control:- A person should control anger and other negative emotions during a conflict.
  7. Negotiation:- The ability to discuss different options and reach a mutually acceptable agreement is important.
  8. Open-Mindedness:- A person should be willing to consider viewpoints different from their own.
  9. Fairness:- All people involved should be treated fairly and respectfully.

These skills help a person manage conflicts peacefully and find solutions that are acceptable to the people involved.

6. Define stress. Explain the different types of stress.

Answer:-
Stress is a physical, mental, or emotional response to demanding or challenging situations. It occurs when a person feels that the demands of a situation are difficult to handle.

Stress can be positive or negative depending on its nature, intensity, and duration.

Types of stress:-

1. Eustress:-
Eustress is positive or useful stress. It can motivate a person and improve performance.

Example:- A student feels slightly nervous before an examination and uses that feeling as motivation to study.

2. Distress:-
Distress is harmful or excessive stress. It can negatively affect a person's well-being and performance.

Example:- Continuous examination anxiety causes a student to lose sleep and concentration.

3. Acute Stress:-
Acute stress is short-term stress caused by a particular event or situation.

Example:- Nervousness before giving a presentation.

4. Chronic Stress:-
Chronic stress is long-term stress that continues for a prolonged period.

Example:- Continuous work pressure for several months.

Thus, understanding different types of stress helps a person recognise stress and manage it appropriately.

7. What is self-awareness about stress? Explain the signs of stress.

Answer:-
Self-awareness about stress means recognising one's own thoughts, emotions, physical reactions, and behaviour when experiencing stress. It helps a person identify stress at an early stage and take appropriate action.

Signs of stress:-

1. Physical signs:-

  • Headache.
  • Tiredness.
  • Muscle tension.
  • Sleep problems.
  • Increased heart rate.

2. Emotional signs:-

  • Irritability.
  • Anxiety.
  • Frustration.
  • Feeling overwhelmed.

3. Behavioural signs:-

  • Avoiding responsibilities.
  • Changes in eating habits.
  • Poor concentration.
  • Social withdrawal.

Importance of self-awareness about stress:-

  • Recognise stress early.
  • Take appropriate action.
  • Prevent excessive stress.
  • Improve productivity.
  • Maintain emotional balance.
  • Protect relationships.

Therefore, understanding one's own stress responses is an important part of effective stress management.

8. Explain different methods of regulating stress.

Answer:-
Stress regulation means managing and controlling our responses to stressful situations so that stress does not negatively affect our physical, mental, and emotional well-being.

Some important methods of regulating stress are:-

1. Time Management:-
Planning tasks and setting priorities can reduce unnecessary pressure.

2. Exercise:-
Regular physical activity can help reduce tension and improve mood.

3. Proper Sleep:-
Adequate and regular sleep helps the body and mind recover from stress.

4. Relaxation:-
Deep breathing, meditation, and other relaxation techniques can help calm the mind.

5. Positive Thinking:-
Instead of focusing only on negative possibilities, a person should maintain a realistic attitude and focus on possible solutions.

6. Social Support:-
Talking to trusted friends, family members, or supportive people can help a person deal with stressful situations.

7. Breaking Tasks into Smaller Parts:-
A large or difficult task can be divided into smaller steps. This makes the task easier to manage.

8. Healthy Lifestyle:-
A balanced diet, adequate sleep, regular exercise, and healthy daily habits can support overall well-being.

Thus, effective stress regulation helps a person remain calm, productive, and emotionally balanced.

9. How can stress be converted into a positive force?

Answer:-
Not all stress is harmful. A moderate amount of stress can sometimes increase alertness, concentration, energy, and motivation. The important thing is to regulate stress instead of allowing stress to control us.

Stress can be converted into a positive force in the following ways:-

  1. Change negative thinking:- Instead of thinking, "I will definitely fail," think, "I am nervous because this is important, so I will prepare properly."
  2. Use stress as motivation:- A certain amount of pressure can encourage a person to work harder and complete tasks on time.
  3. Prepare properly:- When stress reminds us that something is important, we can use it as motivation to prepare better.
  4. Set clear goals:- Breaking a difficult task into smaller goals makes it easier to manage.
  5. Stay organised:- Proper planning and time management can turn pressure into productive action.
  6. Maintain a positive attitude:- Focusing on solutions instead of constantly worrying can make stressful situations easier to handle.

Example:-

Before an examination, a student may feel nervous.

❌ Negative response:- "I will definitely fail."

✅ Positive response:-

"I am nervous because the examination is important. I will use this energy to prepare properly."

Therefore, stress can become a positive force when it is understood, controlled, and used as motivation for preparation and improvement.
